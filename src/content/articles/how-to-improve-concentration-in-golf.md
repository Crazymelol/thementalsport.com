How to improve concentration in golf: you need to develop the ability to switch your focus on and off between shots, conserve attention over four-plus hours, and maintain a consistent pre-shot routine. This allows you to recharge and refocus for each shot. Golf demands short bursts of intense focus, then brief periods of relaxation.

## What is Focus in Golf?
Focus in golf is the ability to concentrate on the present moment, without distractions, and execute each shot to the best of your ability. It's not about being focused for the entire round, but rather switching your focus on and off between shots. You need to be able to turn your attention on, make a clear decision, and commit to your shot, then turn it off and recharge for the next one.

### Switching Focus On and Off
To improve your concentration in golf, you need to develop a switch-on/switch-off focus protocol. This involves turning your attention on before each shot, making a clear decision, and committing to your shot, then turning it off and relaxing until your next shot. You can do this by taking a few deep breaths, feeling your feet on the ground, and focusing on the sensation of the club in your hands.

## Pre-Shot Routine
A consistent pre-shot routine is essential for maintaining focus and concentration in golf. This routine should be the same for every shot, and should include a series of physical and mental actions that help you prepare for the shot. For example, you might take a few practice swings, align your feet and shoulders, and visualize the ball flying straight and true. You can learn more about building a pre-game routine on our [blog about how to build a pre-game routine](/blog/how-to-build-a-pre-game-routine).

### Staying Present After a Bad Hole
One of the biggest challenges in golf is staying present and focused after a bad hole. It's easy to get caught up in negative thoughts and emotions, and to let your mind wander to all the things that could go wrong. But this can lead to a downward spiral of poor play and frustration. Instead, you need to learn to stay present and focused, even when things aren't going your way. You can do this by taking a few deep breaths, feeling your feet on the ground, and focusing on the sensation of the club in your hands. You can also try using self-distanced self-talk, a technique developed by Ethan Kross, to talk to yourself in a more rational and supportive way.

## Finishing the Round Mentally
The final few holes of a round can be mentally challenging, especially if you're tired or frustrated. But it's essential to stay focused and motivated, and to finish the round with a strong mental game. You can do this by setting small goals for yourself, such as making a few solid shots or sinking a putt. You can also try using positive self-talk, and reminding yourself of your strengths and abilities. For more information on mental performance in golf, check out our [guide to mental performance in golf](/mental-performance/golf).

### Step-by-Step Protocol
Here is a step-by-step protocol for improving your concentration in golf:
1. Develop a consistent pre-shot routine that includes physical and mental actions to prepare for each shot.
2. Practice switching your focus on and off between shots, using deep breaths and physical sensations to help you stay present.
3. Use self-distanced self-talk to stay rational and supportive, even when things aren't going your way.
4. Set small goals for yourself, such as making a few solid shots or sinking a putt, to stay motivated and focused.
5. Take breaks and practice relaxation techniques, such as deep breathing or meditation, to help you recharge and refocus.
