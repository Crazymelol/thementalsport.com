If you’re wondering **how to sleep before a competition**, follow this five‑step NSDR cognitive wind‑down protocol. It quiets the mind, lowers arousal, and lets you drift off even when anxiety spikes.

## Why one night of fragmented sleep rarely hurts performance  
Research shows that a single night of broken sleep does not meaningfully reduce maximal strength or aerobic capacity. The body’s muscular glycogen stores and neural drive stay intact. What does damage performance is the mental narrative that “I’m ruined because I didn’t sleep.” Stress hormones rise, perception of effort inflates, and you enter the arena already fatigued. The remedy is to break that narrative, not to chase perfect sleep.

## What NSDR is and why athletes use it  
Non‑Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) is a guided relaxation state that mimics the brain waves of early sleep while you remain awake. Andrew Huberman explains that a 10‑minute NSDR session can drop cortisol, increase parasympathetic tone, and improve focus. For athletes, that means a calmer pre‑game mind and a body ready to fire on demand.

## NSDR Cognitive Wind‑Down Protocol (5 steps)  

1. **Set the environment** – Dim the lights, silence notifications, and cool the room to around 65 °F. A consistent cue tells your nervous system it’s time to shut down.  
2. **Physiological sigh** – Inhale deeply through the nose for a count of four, add a second quick inhale, then exhale slowly through the mouth for eight seconds. Huberman notes this simple pattern instantly reduces heart rate.  
3. **Guided NSDR audio** – Play a 10‑minute NSDR script (many are free on streaming platforms). Follow the voice as it leads you through body scanning and breath awareness.  
4. **Self‑distanced self‑talk** – After the audio, pause and address your worries from a third‑person perspective. “You are nervous about tomorrow’s race, but the body is prepared.” Ethan Kross shows this technique dampens emotional reactivity.  
5. **Growth‑mindset affirmation** – End with a short statement like, “I will adapt to any sleep level and still perform at my best.” Carol Dweck’s work links such framing to resilient performance under pressure.

Complete the protocol within an hour of lights‑out. If you wake after the session, repeat the physiological sigh and a quick mental reset before returning to sleep.

## Pairing the protocol with other pre‑game habits  

- **Nutrition** – Finish heavy meals at least three hours before bed. A light snack of carbs and protein can stabilize blood sugar without causing digestive discomfort.  
- **Light movement** – Gentle stretching or yoga for five minutes releases muscular tension. Avoid high‑intensity work after 8 p.m.; it spikes adrenaline.  
- **Screen curfew** – Blue light suppresses melatonin. Switch to amber lenses or read a printed playbook instead of scrolling.  
- **Breathing rhythm** – Incorporate the 4‑7‑8 pattern during the NSDR audio: inhale four seconds, hold seven, exhale eight. The ratio further activates the vagus nerve.  
- **Mental rehearsal** – Visualize a successful competition in vivid detail, then mentally step back and observe the scene. This blends imagery with the self‑distanced approach, keeping the brain engaged but not overstimulated.

## Common mistakes that sabotage sleep before a competition  

- **Caffeine after 2 p.m.** – Even small doses linger in the system and interfere with the physiological sigh’s calming effect.  
- **Over‑thinking the protocol** – Trying to force sleep creates tension. Trust the process; let the brain follow the script.  
- **Skipping the sigh** – Without that quick autonomic reset, heart rate stays elevated, making the NSDR audio harder to settle into.  
- **Ignoring the narrative** – If you dismiss the mental story that “I can’t perform without perfect sleep,” the stress loop reactivates each night.

## Quick tools you can use tonight  

- A timer set for 10 minutes to cue the NSDR audio.  
- A smartphone app that offers a blue‑light filter after sunset.  
- A notebook for a one‑sentence self‑distanced note (e.g., “You feel nervous, but the body is ready”).  

Apply the five steps, respect the environment, and you’ll notice a smoother transition into rest. The brain’s ability to down‑regulate stress is powerful; you just need to give it the right signals.

**Takeaway:** Sleep anxiety hurts more than a few fragmented hours. Use the NSDR Cognitive Wind‑Down Protocol, add a physiological sigh, and reframe your thoughts.
