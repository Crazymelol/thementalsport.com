The physiological sigh is a breathing technique that can calm you down in seconds, making it a valuable tool for athletes who need to drop their heart rate quickly before competing. This technique involves a double inhale followed by a long exhale. By using the physiological sigh, you can calm your body faster than with a slow breath.

## What is the Physiological Sigh?
The physiological sigh is a specific breathing pattern that was popularized by Andrew Huberman at Stanford. It involves taking two quick inhales through your nose, followed by a long exhale through your mouth. This pattern can help to calm your nervous system and slow down your heart rate.

### How Does it Work?
When you take the two quick inhales, you are stimulating your sympathetic nervous system, which is responsible for your body's "fight or flight" response. However, by following these inhales with a long exhale, you are activating your parasympathetic nervous system, which helps to calm your body down. This rapid shift from sympathetic to parasympathetic dominance can help to calm your body quickly.

## When to Use the Physiological Sigh
You can use the physiological sigh in any situation where you need to calm down quickly, such as before a big game or competition. It can also be used during competition, such as between points or during a timeout. To get the most out of the physiological sigh, use it in conjunction with other techniques, such as [athlete meditation](/blog/athlete-meditation-guide) or strategies to [stop being nervous before a game](/blog/how-to-stop-being-nervous-before-a-game).

### Step-by-Step Protocol
Here is a step-by-step guide to using the physiological sigh:
1. Take two quick inhales through your nose, filling your lungs completely.
2. Hold your breath for a brief moment, about one second.
3. Exhale slowly through your mouth, emptying your lungs completely.
4. Repeat this process two to three times, or as needed.
5. As you exhale, focus on relaxing your muscles and calming your mind.

## Making it a Cue
To get the most out of the physiological sigh, make it a cue by using it consistently in situations where you need to calm down. With practice, your body will learn to associate the physiological sigh with feelings of calmness and relaxation. You can also use the physiological sigh in combination with other techniques, such as self-distanced self-talk or a growth mindset, to enhance its effectiveness.
