What to say before tryouts is just as important as what you say after a game. You want to take pressure off your child, focus on effort not selection. Saying "go out there and give it your all" is a good start.

## Pre-Tryout Pressure
You're driving your kid to tryouts, and the car is filled with tension. Your child is nervous, and you want to help them relax. Avoid saying "you have to make this team" as it adds unnecessary pressure. Instead, focus on the process, not the outcome. Remind them that tryouts are an opportunity to learn and improve.

### Taking Pressure Off
To take pressure off, remind your child that it's okay to make mistakes. Emphasize that you're proud of them regardless of the outcome. You can say something like "I know you're nervous, but I'm proud of you for trying out. Remember, it's okay to make mistakes, and we'll learn from them together." This helps shift the focus from selection to effort.

## The Pre-Tryout Script
Here's a step-by-step script to help you prepare your child for tryouts:
1. Start with a positive affirmation: "You've worked hard for this, and I'm proud of you."
2. Focus on effort, not outcome: "Remember, it's not about making the team, it's about giving it your all and having fun."
3. Remind them to breathe and relax: "Take a deep breath, and let's get excited for this opportunity to learn and improve."
4. End with a reassuring statement: "No matter what happens, I'll be here to support you, and we'll work together to improve for next time."

For more tips on how to support your child in sports, check out our [sports parents playbook](/blog/sports-parents-playbook). 

## What to Say if They Don't Make It
If your child doesn't make the team, it's essential to be supportive and encouraging. Avoid saying "it's not a big deal" or "you'll get them next time" as it can come across as dismissive. Instead, acknowledge their feelings and remind them that it's okay to be disappointed. You can say something like "I know you're upset, and it's okay to feel that way. We'll work together to find another opportunity for you to play and improve." For more guidance on how to handle these situations, consider reading "Nurturing Self-Worth" by Giannis Notaras.
