Perfectionism in sports can be a major obstacle to performance, as it often leads to fear of mistakes and cautious play. You may think that striving for perfection is a good thing, but in reality, it can hold you back from reaching your full potential. Perfectionism in sports is a mindset that needs to be addressed in order to play freely and confidently.

## What is Perfectionism in Sports?
Perfectionism is often misunderstood as having high standards, but in reality, it's a fear-driven mindset that can cause athletes to become tight and tentative. There's a difference between good perfectionism, which is about striving for excellence, and bad perfectionism, which is about avoiding mistakes at all costs. Good perfectionism is about pushing yourself to be better, while bad perfectionism is about being afraid of not being perfect.

### The Problem with Perfectionism
When you're driven by perfectionism, one mistake can unravel your entire game. You become so focused on avoiding errors that you lose sight of the process and the present moment. This can lead to a cycle of self-doubt and criticism, which can be difficult to break. You need to learn to distinguish between striving for excellence and striving for perfection.

## Chasing Excellence, Not Perfection
Instead of striving for perfection, you should focus on chasing excellence. This means focusing on the process and the effort you put in, rather than the outcome. When you focus on the process, you can free yourself from the fear of mistakes and play more freely. This is where the concept of [process goals vs outcome goals](/blog/process-goals-vs-outcome-goals) comes in. By setting process goals, you can focus on what you can control and let go of the need for perfection.

## The Excellence-Not-Perfection Reframe
Here's a step-by-step protocol to help you reframe your mindset:
1. Identify your perfectionistic thoughts: become aware of when you're thinking "I need to be perfect" or "I'm not good enough".
2. Challenge those thoughts: ask yourself if they're really true, and if they're helping or hindering your performance.
3. Reframe your thoughts: replace perfectionistic thoughts with excellence-oriented thoughts, such as "I'm going to give it my best effort" or "I'm going to focus on the process".
4. Focus on the present moment: instead of worrying about the outcome, focus on what you can control in the present moment.
5. Practice self-compassion: treat yourself with kindness and understanding, just as you would a friend.

## Stopping Choking Under Pressure
Perfectionism can also lead to choking under pressure. When you're afraid of making mistakes, you can become tight and tentative, which can lead to poor performance. To overcome this, you need to learn how to [stop choking under pressure](/blog/how-to-stop-choking-under-pressure). This involves developing strategies for managing your nerves and staying focused under pressure.
