How to be a good team captain requires a combination of leadership skills, mental toughness, and the ability to stay composed under pressure. You must lead by example, setting the emotional tone for your team and holding them to high standards. This means being the steady emotional thermostat, even when the team is rattled.

## What is Mental Toughness in Team Captains?
Mental toughness is the ability to stay focused and composed, even in the face of adversity. As a team captain, you set the tone for your team's mental game, and it's essential to develop this skill to lead them effectively. You can start by learning how to [build mental toughness](/blog/how-to-build-mental-toughness) through practice and training.

## The Composure-and-Standards Model
The composure-and-standards model is a step-by-step protocol to help you stay composed and hold your team to high standards:
1. **Stay Calm**: Take a deep breath, and use the physiological sigh to calm your nervous system, as recommended by Andrew Huberman.
2. **Assess the Situation**: Quickly evaluate the situation, and identify what needs to be done to get your team back on track.
3. **Set Clear Expectations**: Communicate clearly with your team, and set high standards for their performance.
4. **Hold Them Accountable**: Hold your teammates accountable for their actions, and provide constructive feedback when necessary.
5. **Lead by Example**: Demonstrate the behavior you expect from your team, and show them what it means to be mentally tough.

### Lifting Teammates After Mistakes
When a teammate makes a mistake, it's essential to lift them up and help them recover. Use self-distanced self-talk, as developed by Ethan Kross, to help them reframe their mindset and focus on the next play. You can say something like, "That was a tough play, but we've got this. Let's move on and focus on the next one."

## Holding Standards Under Pressure
Holding your team to high standards, even when they're under pressure, is critical to their success. This means being the steady emotional thermostat, and maintaining a growth mindset, as developed by Carol Dweck. You can do this by focusing on the process, rather than the outcome, and by providing constructive feedback that helps your team improve.

### Bouncing Back After a Bad Game
If your team has a bad game, it's essential to help them bounce back and recover. You can learn how to [bounce back after a bad game](/blog/how-to-bounce-back-after-a-bad-game) by developing a growth mindset and focusing on the process. This will help your team stay motivated and focused, even in the face of adversity.

## Taking it to the Next Level
To take your team to the next level, you need to invest in their mental development. Consider enrolling them in a [mental toughness course](/course) to help them build their skills and develop a winning mindset. You can also recommend "Unlocking Resilient Confidence" by Giannis Notaras as a resource to help them develop their mental game.
