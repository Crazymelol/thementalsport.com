Balancing school and sport requires careful planning to avoid burnout, especially in September when the school year and sports season collide. You need to prioritize your time, focus on your goals, and learn to say no to non-essential activities. By creating a weekly schedule that includes dedicated time for schoolwork, practice, and rest, you can maintain a healthy balance between academics and athletics.

## What is Burnout?
Burnout is a state of emotional, mental, and physical exhaustion caused by excessive and prolonged stress. It can result in decreased motivation, reduced performance, and increased risk of injury or illness. As a student athlete, you are at risk of burnout due to the demands of both school and sport. Recognizing the warning signs of burnout, such as chronic fatigue, irritability, and decreased performance, is crucial to taking steps to prevent it.

## Creating a Weekly Structure
To balance school and sport, you need to create a weekly structure that includes time for schoolwork, practice, and rest. Start by identifying your priorities: schoolwork, practice, and rest. Allocate specific times for each activity, and stick to your schedule as much as possible. For example, you may dedicate Monday to Wednesday for schoolwork, Thursday for practice, and Friday for rest. You can also use the [sports parents playbook](/blog/sports-parents-playbook) to help you manage your time and prioritize your activities.

### Protecting Sleep
Sleep is essential for physical and mental recovery, and it is especially important for student athletes. Aim for 8-10 hours of sleep each night to help your body and mind recover from the demands of school and sport. Establish a consistent sleep schedule, and create a sleep-conducive environment to improve the quality of your sleep. Avoid screens and electronic devices before bedtime, and try to relax before sleep by reading a book, such as "The ADHD Athlete's Edge" by Giannis Notaras, or listening to calming music.

## The Weekly Load Plan
The weekly load plan is a step-by-step protocol to help you manage your time and prioritize your activities. Here's how to create a weekly load plan:
1. Identify your priorities: schoolwork, practice, and rest.
2. Allocate specific times for each activity.
3. Schedule time for self-care, such as exercise, meditation, or spending time with friends and family.
4. Review and adjust your schedule regularly to ensure it is working for you.
5. Learn to say no to non-essential activities that can interfere with your priorities.

## When to Drop Something
Sometimes, you may need to drop something to maintain a healthy balance between school and sport. If you find that you are consistently struggling to keep up with your schoolwork or practice, it may be necessary to drop a non-essential activity or adjust your schedule. For example, you may need to drop a club or extracurricular activity to focus on your schoolwork or practice. Remember to communicate with your coaches, teachers, and parents about your needs and limitations.

## Warning Signs of Overload
It's essential to recognize the warning signs of overload, such as:
* Chronic fatigue or exhaustion
* Decreased motivation or interest in activities
* Reduced performance in school or sport
* Increased risk of injury or illness
* Irritability or mood swings
If you or your child is experiencing any of these warning signs, it's crucial to take steps to address the issue. You can use the [what to say after a game](/blog/what-to-say-after-a-game) guide to help you communicate with your child and provide support.

## Take Control of Your Time
To balance school and sport, you need to take control of your time and prioritize your activities. By creating a weekly structure, protecting your sleep, and using the weekly load plan, you can maintain a healthy balance between academics and athletics. Remember to stay flexible and adjust your schedule as needed. You can also take our [course](/course) to learn more about managing your time and prioritizing your activities.
