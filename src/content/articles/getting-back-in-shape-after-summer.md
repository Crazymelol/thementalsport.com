Getting back to training after summer feels like stepping onto a stage where everyone’s already performing. The real obstacle isn’t the lost mileage or the weaker lifts; it’s the ego hit of seeing yourself worse than you remember. Accept the rust, focus on tiny, consistent reps, and the shame spiral will lose its grip.

## Why does the ego sting more than the muscles?

Your brain stores a performance snapshot from the last season. When you return, that snapshot clashes with the current reality. The mismatch triggers a self‑critical voice that can shut you down faster than a sore hamstring. Studies show that self‑distanced self‑talk, talking to yourself as if you were an observer, reduces that inner critic. Imagine watching a teammate stumble; you’d offer encouragement, not condemnation. Apply the same lens to yourself.

## How to reset expectations and quit the shame spiral

1. **Compare to yesterday, not last year.** Write down the number of push‑ups, the distance you jog, or the weight you lift each day. The only valid benchmark is the previous entry.  
2. **Name the feeling.** When shame appears, label it: “I’m feeling ashamed of my speed.” Naming emotions creates psychological distance, weakening their power.  
3. **Set micro‑goals.** Instead of “run a 5K in 20 minutes,” aim for “run 5 minutes without stopping.” Small wins generate dopamine, reinforcing the habit loop.  

If you catch yourself spiraling, pause the workout, breathe a physiological sigh (a double inhale followed by a long exhale, as described by Andrew Huberman), then return with a fresh intent. The sigh resets the nervous system, giving you a momentary calm to break the negative loop.

## What is the two‑week restart ladder?

The two‑week restart ladder is a step‑by‑step protocol that builds capacity while protecting ego. You start with a fraction of your previous load and add a fixed increment each session. The ladder is designed to keep you in the “just‑right” zone, hard enough to matter, easy enough to stay consistent.

### Step‑by‑step protocol

| Day | Activity | Load | Reps | Increment |
|-----|----------|------|------|-----------|
| 1   | Bodyweight squat | 30 % of last season’s max | 8 | +5 % |
| 2   | Light jog | 10 % of usual mileage | 10 min | +5 min |
| 3   | Rest or mobility | – | – | – |
| 4   | Push‑up | 30 % of max reps | 6 | +2 reps |
| 5   | Bike or swim | 10 % of usual time | 12 min | +4 min |
| 6   | Rest or yoga | – | – | – |
| 7   | Full body circuit | 35 % of last season’s intensity | 2 rounds | +1 round |
| 8‑14| Repeat days 1‑7, adding the listed increment each session | – | – | – |

Key points:

* **Consistency beats intensity.** Missing a day resets the ladder; skipping a session repeatedly erodes confidence.  
* **Stay within 10‑15 % of the previous day’s load.** Anything larger feels like a setback and fuels shame.  
* **Log every session.** Seeing the ladder climb on paper is proof that you are improving, even if the numbers look tiny.

After two weeks you’ll have a clear picture of where you stand. From there you can either extend the ladder or transition to a regular periodized program.

## How to keep momentum after week two

The ladder’s purpose is to create a habit foundation. Once you’ve completed it, reinforce the habit with three practices:

1. **Pre‑performance routine.** A short, repeatable sequence, breathing, visualisation, a cue phrase, preps your brain for action. Check out our guide on how to build a pre‑game routine for a template you can adapt.  
2. **Self‑talk audit.** Record the inner dialogue that appears during a hard set. Replace “I’m weak” with “I’m getting stronger each rep.” This mirrors the approach in the book *Unlocking Resilient Confidence* by Giannis Notaras, which outlines practical confidence drills.  
3. **Skill focus day.** Dedicate one session per week to a technical element you enjoy. Mastery in a small area fuels overall motivation and prevents burnout.

## Tools to support your comeback

* **Progress journal.** A simple notebook or app where you note load, reps, mood, and any shame triggers. Review it weekly to spot patterns.  
* **Accountability partner.** Share your ladder plan with a teammate or coach. Knowing someone else sees your increments adds a layer of external validation.  
* **Educational resources.** If pressure spikes during competition, the article on how to stop choking under pressure offers concrete steps to stay calm.  
* **Structured learning.** Enroll in our course for a deeper dive into mental resilience and performance planning. The curriculum aligns with the ladder’s philosophy, ensuring you don’t outgrow the habit too quickly.

## Bottom‑line takeaways

* Ego pain is the real barrier; treat it like any other injury, acknowledge, treat, and move on.  
* Tiny, progressive reps win over giant, unsustainable jumps.  
* The two‑week restart ladder gives you a roadmap that protects confidence while rebuilding capacity.  
* Pair the ladder with a pre‑performance routine, disciplined self‑talk, and a skill‑focus day to lock in long‑term momentum.

Stay honest with yourself, trust the process, and you’ll find the rust melts faster than you think.
