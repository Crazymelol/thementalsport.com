# How to Find Strangers Worth Messaging

The problem with "send DMs" is finding the people. Here is where they actually
are, in order of how well it works.

---

## 1. Followers of youth sports clubs (best source)

Sports parents follow their kid's club. The club account is a list of your exact
buyers, already gathered for you.

**Method:**
1. Instagram search: `soccer academy athens`, `fencing club [city]`, `swim club
   [city]`, `basketball academy [city]`, `gymnastics [city]`.
2. Open a club account, tap **Followers**.
3. Scroll. Parents are obvious: real name, kid in the profile photo, bio like
   "mum of 2" or a team name.
4. Message the ones whose recent posts show a kid competing.

Do this for clubs in any city, not just yours. This is digital.

**Your unfair advantage:** you are a fencing coach. You already know fencing club
accounts across Greece and abroad, and they will recognise your name. Start
there, then widen to other sports.

---

## 2. Hashtags, sorted by RECENT not top

Recent posters are active right now. Top posts are big accounts who ignore you.

Search these and open **Recent**:
- `#youthsports` `#youthsoccer` `#youthbasketball` `#travelball`
- `#sportsparents` `#soccermom` `#hockeymom` `#swimparent`
- `#tryouts` `#preseason` `#studentathlete`
- Greek: `#ακαδημια` `#ξιφασκια` `#στιβος`

Anyone posting "tryouts tomorrow", "first practice back", "so proud of her
today" is a live target. Their kid is in the exact moment your content is about.

---

## 3. People who COMMENT on big accounts in your niche

A follower is passive. A commenter is engaged and easy to start a conversation
with, because you have something to reply to.

Find large sports-psychology / youth-sports accounts, open their recent posts,
read the comments. Anyone asking a question is the best possible target: answer
their question properly in a DM, for free, with no pitch at all.

---

## 4. Facebook groups (highest intent, often better than Instagram)

Search Facebook for: `youth soccer parents`, `[sport] parents`, `travel team
parents`, `[city] sports parents`. Many have tens of thousands of members and
constant posts from parents describing exactly the problems you solve.

Rule: **answer questions in the group, do not link.** Post the whole protocol as
a comment. People will visit your profile. That is the click you want.

---

## 5. Reddit

`r/youthsports` `r/soccercoach` `r/basketballcoach` `r/Fencing` `r/swimming`
`r/tennis`. Sort by New. Answer questions in full, no link. Same rule.

---

## Never message minors

This is the one hard rule. Do not DM anyone who looks like a child or teenager,
ever. It is wrong, it will get the account reported, and they cannot buy anything
anyway. The buyer is always the parent or the coach.

**Kid-facing accounts attract kids.** A big academy posting "TRYOUTS ARE OPEN"
gets a comment section full of 13-year-olds. Do not source names from there.

**How to tell them apart in two seconds:**

| Signal | Kid account | Parent account |
|---|---|---|
| Profile photo | Young face, action shot in kit | Adult, or family, or their kid |
| Username | Nickname, jersey number, birth year (`_07`, `x`, `24`) | Real first + last name |
| Bio | School, team, position, "D1 hopeful" | "Mum of 2", job, city, family |
| Posts | Highlights of themselves | Their child playing, family, life |

If you are unsure, skip. There are plenty of clearly-adult accounts.

## Also skip

- Big brand and influencer accounts (they get 100 DMs a day and ignore all).
- Accounts with no posts or no profile photo.
- Private accounts with no visible context. No context, no message.

## Where the PARENTS actually are

Instagram youth-sports content skews to the players. Parents cluster elsewhere:

1. **Facebook groups.** This is the single best parent channel and there are
   almost no minors in them. Search `youth soccer parents`, `[sport] parents`,
   `travel team parents`, `[city] sports parents`.
2. **Local club accounts**, where parents follow for schedules and logistics.
3. **Parent-signal hashtags:** `#soccermom` `#hockeymom` `#sportsmom`
   `#sportsdad` `#sportsparents`. These are written by parents, about parents.
4. **Comments on parent-facing content** (car ride home, sideline behaviour,
   burnout) rather than player-facing content (tryouts, highlights, drills).

For your athlete-facing content, adults and coaches are still fair game. Just
never a child.

---

## The message

Never paste the same text twice. Instagram detects identical messages and
throttles the account. Change the first line every time using something real from
their profile.

**Template (adapt line 1 each time):**
> Hey [Name], saw [specific thing: your daughter's tryout post / you coach the
> U14s]. I'm a fencing coach and I write about the mental side of sport, mostly
> for parents.
>
> Made a free 2-minute quiz that finds the one mental weak spot costing an
> athlete games. No signup to see the result: thementalsport.com/quiz
>
> Hope it helps.

Then **stop**. Do not follow up with a pitch. If they reply, have a real
conversation. That is it.

---

## Safety limits (important)

New accounts that blast DMs get restricted, and it is hard to undo.

- **Day 1-3:** 10-15 DMs a day, maximum.
- **Week 2:** up to 20-25.
- Never more than about 30 a day to people who do not follow you.
- Space them out. Do not send 20 in five minutes.
- If Instagram shows "Action Blocked", stop completely for 48 hours.

Follow + engage with a couple of their posts before messaging. It looks human,
because it is.

---

## The daily target

**20 people a day.** Ten from a club's follower list, ten from recent hashtag
posts. Fifteen minutes.

Track only this: `DMs sent | replies | quiz takers`. After a week you will know
which source produces replies, and you drop the other one.
