## 1. The Attitude That Decides Your Season
*(card: the-attitude-that-decides-your-season.png)*

> Your season gets decided in the first two weeks. Not in the final.
>
> Not by talent. By the standard you accept when nobody's watching and you're
> unfit and it's 35 degrees and you'd rather be at the beach.
>
> Set it now, while it's still easy to be honest with yourself:
> What are you not willing to skip this year?
>
> Write down three things. Those are your non-negotiables. Everything else is
> just motivation, and motivation runs out in October.

## 2. What to Say to Your Child Before Tryouts
*(card: what-to-say-to-your-child-before-tryouts.png)*

> The worst thing you can say in the car before tryouts is "just do your best."
>
> Kids hear that as pressure. Their best is exactly what they're afraid they
> can't deliver.
>
> Say this instead: "Go and enjoy competing. I'm glad I get to watch."
>
> That's it. No advice. No reminders about the thing you talked about last week.
> They already know. Your job in that car is to lower their heart rate, not
> raise it.
>
> [your line: what you say to your own kid before they compete]

## 3. How to Handle Tryout Nerves
*(card: how-to-handle-tryout-nerves.png)*

> Coaches don't cut nervous players. They cut invisible ones.
>
> Nerves make you play safe. You stop asking for the ball. You take the easy
> pass instead of the right one. You disappear, and the coach never gets to see
> what you can actually do.
>
> So pick one thing you'll do regardless of how you feel:
> Ask for the ball every time it's on. Sprint every recovery. Talk loudest.
>
> You can't control whether you're nervous. You can control whether you're seen.

## 4. What If My Child Doesn't Make the Team
*(card: what-if-my-child-doesnt-make-the-team.png)*

> If your kid gets cut this month, do nothing for 24 hours.
>
> Don't fix it. Don't call the coach. Don't tell them there's always next year.
> Don't say it's the coach's loss. Every one of those tells them their feeling
> is a problem to be solved.
>
> Let them be gutted. Sit with them.
>
> The next day, one question: "Do you want to work on it?"
>
> If yes, you've got a plan. If no, you've still got a kid who trusts you. Both
> are fine.
>
> Getting cut isn't the damage. Rushing them through it is.

## 5. Getting Your Head Back After a Summer Off
*(card: getting-back-in-shape-after-summer.png)*

> Coming back after a summer off isn't hard because you're unfit.
>
> It's hard because you're worse than you were, and you know it, and everyone
> can see it.
>
> That's the part people quit over. Not the running. The ego.
>
> The fix is boring: go three times before you judge anything. Compare yourself
> to last Tuesday, not to your best self in March. Fitness comes back faster
> than you think. Confidence only comes back if you keep showing up while you're
> still bad.

## 6. How to Get Mentally Ready for Preseason
*(card: how-to-get-mentally-ready-for-preseason.png)*

> Most athletes treat preseason as something to survive.
>
> The ones who move up treat it as the only part of the year where the depth
> chart is genuinely open. Nobody has a position yet. Every session is an
> audition and most of your teammates haven't realised.
>
> Decide now what you want the coach to say about you in week four. Then behave
> like that from day one.

## 7. Balancing School and Sport
*(card: balancing-school-and-sport.png)*

> September is when young athletes break. Not from training. From the collision.
>
> School restarts, season restarts, and the same kid is now doing both at full
> load with less sleep.
>
> Watch for it: they stop wanting to go to training they used to love. They get
> sick more. Small setbacks land hard.
>
> That's overload, not weakness. And the answer is usually one less thing, not
> more discipline.
>
> Parents: it's okay to drop something in September. It's cheaper than dropping
> the sport in January.

---

## 8. First Practice With a New Coach
*(card: first-practice-with-a-new-coach.png)*

> A new coach forms an opinion of you in about two sessions, and it's hard to
> change after that.
>
> They're not really watching your technique. They're watching what you do
> between drills. Whether you're first or last to the line. Whether you take
> feedback without your face falling apart.
>
> You don't need to be the best player in the room on day one. Be the easiest
> one to coach. That gets you minutes while you get better.

## How to post these

- Caption goes in the post. **CTA goes in the first comment**, not the caption.
  Links in captions suppress reach on most platforms, and a bolted-on CTA is the
  single biggest "this is an ad" signal.
- 3-5 hashtags, not 8. Eight looks automated.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour. That is the actual algorithm hack.
- Add `[your line]` wherever it's marked. Your real experience is the one thing
  no competitor can copy.
