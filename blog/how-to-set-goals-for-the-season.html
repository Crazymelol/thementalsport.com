How to Set Goals for the Season (That You Will Actually Keep)

PUBLISHED: 2026-08-28

How to set goals for the season starts with a clear split between what you want to achieve and how you’ll get there. Nail down two or three weekly non‑negotiables, then lock a review loop that forces you to adjust before October rolls in. That’s the only way wishes become results.

Why most season goals fail

You write “win the league” and feel good. The next week you’re stuck in a slump and the goal feels abstract. Without a process attached, the outcome drifts into fantasy. Studies show athletes who pair outcome goals with concrete process steps perform better under pressure.

Outcome goals vs. process goals

  • Outcome goals are the end points: a podium finish, a personal best, a championship.
  • Process goals are the actions you control: daily sprint drills, nutrition timing, mental rehearsal.

Separate them on paper. Highlight the outcome at the top, then list the processes that directly feed it. This visual cue stops you from confusing desire with discipline.

Choose your weekly non‑negotiables

Pick two or three actions that must happen every week, no exceptions. They become the backbone of your season plan.

  1. Skill session – 45 minutes of the specific drill that improves your weak point.
  2. Recovery ritual – a 10‑minute physiological sigh routine (Andrew Huberman) after every training block.
  3. Mental check‑in – 5 minutes of self‑distanced self‑talk (Ethan Kross) to review performance without judgment.

Write them on a sticky note, place it on your locker, and treat them like a contract.

The “Season Goal Split Protocol”

A step‑by‑step system that turns vague wishes into actionable plans.

| Step | Action | Why it matters | |------|--------|----------------| | 1. Define the outcome | Write the exact result you want (e.g., “Top‑3 finish in the regional meet”). | Gives direction. | | 2. List supporting processes | Identify 4‑6 daily or weekly habits that drive the outcome. | Bridges desire and effort. | | 3. Pick non‑negotiables | Choose 2‑3 processes you will never skip. | Guarantees consistency. | | 4. Schedule a weekly review | Every Sunday, spend 15 minutes comparing results to the plan. Adjust non‑negotiables if they’re not delivering. | Keeps the system alive. | | 5. Archive the data | Log numbers, feelings, and any deviations in a simple spreadsheet. | Provides evidence for future cycles. |

Follow the table each week. If a non‑negotiable slips, the review forces a decision: double down, tweak, or replace. No excuses.

Building the review loop

The review is the glue that prevents goals from evaporating in October. Here’s how to run it:

  1. Gather evidence – pull training logs, nutrition sheets, and mental notes.
  2. Score each non‑negotiable – 0 (missed), 1 (partial), 2 (full). Add them up.
  3. Compare to outcome metrics – are you moving toward the season target? If the gap widens, it’s time to recalibrate.
  4. Set the next week’s focus – decide which process needs extra weight. Write it down.

Treat the review like a pre‑game routine; the same mental discipline that prepares you for competition can sharpen your planning. For more on routines, see the pre‑game routine guide.

Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

  • Overloading with outcomes – you can’t chase ten championships at once. Stick to one primary outcome and a couple of secondary ones.
  • Vague processes – “train harder” is meaningless. Replace it with “run 6×200m at 90% effort with 2‑minute rest.”
  • Skipping the review – life gets busy, but a 15‑minute slot is non‑negotiable. It’s the difference between drifting and steering.

If you feel pressure mounting, remember the strategies from the article on stopping choking under pressure. The same mental tools that keep you calm in a clutch moment keep your goal system steady.

Integrating mental confidence

Your physical plan stalls without belief. Giannis Notaras outlines a practical method in Unlocking Resilient Confidence. Apply his confidence drills after each review to reinforce the link between effort and outcome. The result is a feedback loop where success fuels confidence, and confidence fuels success.

Tracking progress without obsession

You don’t need a complex app. A simple notebook works:

  • Page 1: Season outcome and process list.
  • Pages 2‑10: Weekly logs with scores and reflections.
  • Page 11: Quarterly snapshot – note trends, adjust non‑negotiables, celebrate wins.

The goal is clarity, not data overload.

When October hits

October is the season’s turning point. Many athletes abandon their plans because fatigue sets in. Your weekly review should have already built a habit that survives the slump. If the score drops below a pre‑set threshold, trigger a “mid‑season reset” – a 48‑hour deep dive where you strip back to the core non‑negotiables and re‑commit.

Quick checklist

  • [ ] Write a single, measurable outcome.
  • [ ] List 4‑6 supporting processes.
  • [ ] Choose 2‑3 weekly non‑negotiables.
  • [ ] Schedule a Sunday review.
  • [ ] Log scores and adjust.

Follow this checklist every week, and the season’s goals will feel like a roadmap, not a wish list.

Bottom line: Split the end result from the daily grind, lock in a handful of non‑negotiables, and review every week. That’s how you set goals for the season and actually keep them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many outcome goals should I have for a season?+
One primary outcome and up to two secondary ones keep focus sharp without spreading effort too thin.
What if I miss a weekly non‑negotiable?+
Use the review to decide whether to double down, tweak the habit, or replace it. The key is to address the miss immediately.
Can I use a digital app for tracking?+
Yes, but a simple notebook works just as well. The system’s power lies in consistency, not the tool.
How often should I adjust my process goals?+
Adjust only after a review shows they’re not moving you toward the outcome. Frequent changes dilute progress.
