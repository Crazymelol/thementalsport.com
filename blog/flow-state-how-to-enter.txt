16:["$","h3","h3-0",{"children":"1. Challenge-Skill Balance"}]
17:["$","p","p-7",{"children":"Flow occurs when challenge and skill are both high and approximately matched. Too easy = boredom. Too hard = anxiety. The narrow band where you're stretched but capable produces the activation needed for flow."}]
18:["$","p","p-8",{"children":"Implication: you can't enter flow on tasks you've mastered without challenge. You can't enter flow on tasks that overwhelm your current ability. Deliberate skill development and intelligent challenge calibration are prerequisites."}]
19:["$","h3","h3-1",{"children":"2. Clear Goals"}]
1a:["$","p","p-9",{"children":"Flow requires clarity about what success looks like in the next moment. Not outcome goals (\"win the match\") but process goals (\"hit to his backhand on the first ball\")."}]
1b:["$","p","p-10",{"children":"Vague intentions produce diffuse attention. Clear process goals produce focused attention — which is the attentional prerequisite for flow."}]
1c:["$","h3","h3-2",{"children":"3. Unambiguous Feedback"}]
1d:["$","p","p-11",{"children":"Flow states require moment-to-moment feedback that tells the performer whether they're on track. The feedback doesn't need to be positive — it needs to be clear."}]
1e:["$","p","p-12",{"children":"Sports provide natural feedback loops (the ball goes where intended or doesn't, the shot lands or doesn't). This is one reason athletes access flow more easily than professionals in roles with delayed feedback."}]
1f:["$","h3","h3-3",{"children":"4. Concentration Free of Distraction"}]
20:["$","p","p-13",{"children":"Flow can't begin if attention is split. External distractions (crowd, noise, concerns outside the performance) and internal distractions (self-talk about outcomes, worry about other people's perceptions, pain catastrophizing) both block the entry condition."}]

28:["$","p","p-18",{"children":"Flow requires high arousal — not anxiety arousal, but challenge arousal. Athletes who under-activate (flat, low energy) and those who over-activate (anxious, tense) both have reduced flow access."}]
