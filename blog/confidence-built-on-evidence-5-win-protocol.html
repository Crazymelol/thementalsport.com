Confidence Built on Evidence: The 5-Win Protocol

PUBLISHED: 2026-08-01

How to build confidence in sports: it starts with evidence, not applause. You build confidence by focusing on small, controllable wins, not just game outcomes. This approach helps you develop a sense of self-efficacy that lasts.

What is Fragile Confidence?

Confidence that rises and falls with results is fragile. It's based on external validation, like applause or praise from others. When you're on a winning streak, you feel invincible, but one loss can shatter your confidence. This type of confidence is not reliable, as it's dependent on factors outside your control.

Building Confidence on Evidence

To build lasting confidence, you need to focus on evidence-based wins. This means tracking small, achievable victories that you can control. For example, making a certain number of shots in practice, or completing a tough workout. By focusing on these wins, you build a sense of confidence that's based on your own abilities, not just external outcomes.

The 5-Win Protocol

The 5-Win Protocol is a simple, yet powerful tool for building confidence. After every practice or game, take a few minutes to log three to five small wins. These can be things like:

  1. Completed a tough workout without stopping
  2. Made a certain number of shots in practice
  3. Successfully executed a new skill or move
  4. Communicated effectively with teammates
  5. Stayed focused and composed under pressure

To log your wins, follow these steps:

  1. Take out a notebook or open a note-taking app on your phone
  2. Write down the date and the activity you just completed (practice, game, etc.)
  3. List three to five small wins from the activity
  4. Be specific and detailed in your descriptions
  5. Review your log regularly to see your progress and build confidence

You can also use self-distancing self-talk to reinforce your wins and build confidence. By talking to yourself in the third person, you can gain a more objective perspective on your abilities and accomplishments.

Overcoming Setbacks

Even with a strong confidence-building practice, setbacks will still occur. When you have a bad game or practice, it's essential to know how to bounce back. This involves acknowledging your emotions, reframing negative thoughts, and getting back to work. For more on confidence-building strategies, check out confidence-building by Giannis Notaras.

Daily Wins, Lasting Confidence

By focusing on small, controllable wins and logging them in your 5-Win Protocol, you build a bank of evidence that your brain can draw on under pressure. This type of confidence is not based on external validation, but on your own abilities and achievements. With time and practice, you'll develop a sense of confidence that will serve you well in any situation.

Takeaway: confidence built on evidence is the key to lasting success in sports. By logging your small wins and focusing on controllable achievements, you'll develop a sense of self-efficacy that will help you perform at your best, even under pressure.
