Pressure Training: How to Make Practice Harder Than the Game

PUBLISHED: 2026-08-01

Pressure training for athletes involves making practice harder than the game by simulating the stress and pressure of competition. You can achieve this by adding consequences, audiences, fatigue, and scoreboard pressure to your training sessions. This approach helps automate your skills under stress, so they don't choke when it matters.

What is Pressure Training?

Pressure training is a method used to prepare athletes for the stress of competition by replicating the conditions they will face in a game. This type of training helps you develop the mental and physical toughness needed to perform at your best when it counts. You can learn more about why athletes choke under pressure by visiting our blog post on the topic.

Why Do Athletes Need Pressure Training?

Athletes need pressure training because it helps them develop the ability to perform under stress. When you practice without pressure, your skills may not be able to withstand the stress of competition. This is because your brain and body are not used to functioning under pressure, and as a result, your skills can break down. By incorporating pressure into your training, you can build the resilience and mental toughness needed to succeed in high-pressure situations.

How to Add Pressure to Your Training

There are several ways to add pressure to your training sessions. Here are five concrete ways to manufacture pressure:

  1. Consequences: Add consequences to your training sessions, such as having to run laps or do extra drills if you fail to meet a certain standard.
  2. Audiences: Practice in front of an audience, such as teammates, coaches, or even friends and family.
  3. Fatigue: Train when you are tired or fatigued, to simulate the physical demands of competition.
  4. Scoreboard Pressure: Use a scoreboard or a timer to add an extra level of pressure to your training sessions.
  5. Manufactured-Pressure Drills: Create drills that simulate the pressure of competition, such as having to make a certain number of shots in a row or complete a task within a certain time limit.

Implementing Manufactured-Pressure Drills

To implement manufactured-pressure drills, you need to identify the specific skills you want to work on and create drills that simulate the pressure of competition. For example, if you are a basketball player, you could create a drill where you have to make a certain number of free throws in a row, with a timer running and a consequence for missing a shot.

Building Mental Toughness

In addition to pressure training, building mental toughness is also essential for athletes. You can learn more about how to build mental toughness by visiting our blog post on the topic. Mental toughness is the ability to perform at your best even when faced with adversity, and it is a critical component of success in sports.

Putting it All Together

To get the most out of pressure training, you need to combine it with other training methods, such as building mental toughness and developing a pre-performance routine. You can find more information on how to develop a competition protocol in "the-competition-protocol" by Giannis Notaras.

Creating a Training Plan

To create a training plan that incorporates pressure training, you need to identify your goals and develop a plan that simulates the conditions you will face in competition. This plan should include a mix of technical training, physical conditioning, and mental preparation, as well as opportunities to practice under pressure.

Takeaway

You can make your practice sessions harder than the game by adding consequences, audiences, fatigue, and scoreboard pressure. By incorporating these elements into your training, you can build the mental and physical toughness needed to perform at your best when it counts. Remember, the key to success is to simulate the pressure of competition in your training sessions, so that when the game arrives, you are ready.
