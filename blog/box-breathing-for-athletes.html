Box Breathing for Athletes (The 4-4-4-4 Reset)

PUBLISHED: 2026-07-31

Box breathing for athletes is a simple, repeatable technique to steady yourself under pressure. It involves breathing in for 4 seconds, holding for 4 seconds, exhaling for 4 seconds, and holding again for 4 seconds. This 4-4-4-4 pattern helps calm your nervous system.

What is Box Breathing?

Box breathing, also known as the 4-4-4-4 reset, is a breathing technique that can help athletes manage their nerves and perform at their best. The even rhythm of this technique helps to steady the nervous system, allowing you to focus on the task at hand. Unlike the physiological sigh, which is used to quickly drop your heart rate and calm your nervous system, box breathing is used to hold a steady state.

How to Use Box Breathing

To use box breathing, follow these steps:

  1. Find a quiet, comfortable place to sit or stand with your back straight.
  2. Close your eyes and begin to breathe in through your nose for a count of 4 seconds.
  3. Hold your breath for a count of 4 seconds.
  4. Slowly exhale through your mouth for a count of 4 seconds.
  5. Hold your breath again for a count of 4 seconds.
  6. Repeat this cycle for several rounds, feeling your heart rate slow and your mind calm.

Using Box Breathing Pre-Game and Between Points

Box breathing can be used both before a game and between points to help you stay focused and calm. Unlike the physiological sigh for athletes, which is used to quickly calm the nervous system, box breathing is used to maintain a steady state. By using box breathing regularly, you can improve your mental performance and gain a competitive edge.

Comparison to the Physiological Sigh

While both box breathing and the physiological sigh are used to calm the nervous system, they have different uses. The physiological sigh is used to quickly drop your heart rate and calm your nervous system, whereas box breathing is used to hold a steady state. Takeaway

Box breathing is a simple, effective technique that can help you steady yourself under pressure. By following the 4-4-4-4 pattern and using it regularly, you can improve your mental performance and gain a competitive edge. Remember to use box breathing in conjunction with other techniques, such as the physiological sigh, to get the best results.
