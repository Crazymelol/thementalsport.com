How to Beat the Yips in Golf (Stop Choking Over the Ball)

PUBLISHED: 2026-08-04

How to beat the yips in golf: the yips are a result of explicit monitoring, where your conscious mind takes control of a grooved motion. This can happen to any golfer, even experienced ones, and it's not just a matter of nerves or lack of practice. When you're under pressure, your brain's attempt to control the swing or putt can disrupt the smooth motion you've practiced countless times.

What are the Yips?

The yips are a specific type of performance anxiety that affects golfers. They occur when a golfer's explicit monitoring system kicks in, trying to control every aspect of the swing or putt. This can lead to a jerky, unsmooth motion, resulting in a poor shot. The yips are not just a mental issue, but also a physical one, as the muscles tense up in response to the brain's attempt to control the motion.

Understanding the Mechanism

The yips are closely related to the concept of choking under pressure. When you're in a high-pressure situation, your brain's explicit monitoring system can take over, trying to ensure that you make the perfect shot. However, this can lead to a breakdown in your motor skills, resulting in a poor performance. Studies show that this is due to the brain's attempt to control the motion, rather than letting it happen automatically.

The Role of Explicit Monitoring

Explicit monitoring is the process by which your brain consciously controls your movements. While this can be helpful in certain situations, it can also be detrimental to your performance in golf. When you're trying to make a shot, your brain's explicit monitoring system can kick in, trying to control every aspect of the motion. This can lead to the yips, as your brain's attempt to control the motion disrupts the smooth, grooved motion you've practiced.

Fixing the Yips

So, how can you beat the yips in golf? The key is to reduce your explicit monitoring and let your automatic system take over. One way to do this is by using an external target focus. Instead of focusing on the ball, focus on the target you're trying to hit. This can help distract your brain from the motion itself, allowing your automatic system to take over.

Quiet-Eye Routine

Another technique that can help is a quiet-eye routine. This involves focusing your eyes on the target for a few seconds before making the shot. This can help calm your brain and reduce explicit monitoring. Here's a step-by-step protocol for a quiet-eye routine:

  1. Stand behind the ball and take a few deep breaths.
  2. Focus your eyes on the target, taking in every detail.
  3. Hold your gaze for 2-3 seconds, taking a moment to calm your mind.
  4. Take a deep breath and make the shot, using a hold cue word to help you stay focused. A hold cue word is a word or phrase that you repeat to yourself as you make the shot, helping to keep your mind focused and your explicit monitoring system at bay.

Practicing Under Pressure

To overcome the yips, you need to practice under pressure. This can be done on the range, where you can simulate high-pressure situations and practice your quiet-eye routine and external target focus. You can also try pressure practice on the range to help you build confidence and reduce your explicit monitoring.

Building Confidence

Building confidence is key to overcoming the yips. When you're confident in your abilities, you're less likely to experience explicit monitoring and the yips. You can build confidence by practicing regularly and focusing on your strengths, rather than your weaknesses. As you work to overcome the yips, remember that it's a process that takes time and practice. Don't get discouraged if you don't see immediate results. With patience and persistence, you can overcome the yips and improve your game.
